Why paper fails

A discharge sheet does its job for exactly one ride home.

After that, it's folded into a bag, left on a nightstand, thrown away or lost, right at the moment when a patient needs the information the most — such as when to remember which medication comes at 2 PM.

Postopify replaces the sheet with a link every phone already knows how to open, so the instructions are still there on day three, not just day one. The practice writes them once; the patient can't misplace them.

How it works

Four steps, and no software to set up.

01

Add your procedures

Drop a Google Doc into your practice's folder for each procedure. The title becomes the name patients see.

02

Hand out the code

Each practice sets each patient up with their very own code that is either emailed to them or they scan while waiting in the lobby, with their medication and post-operative instructions individually set. This includes your clinic's name and logo.

03

The patient scans it

The code opens a page built for their procedure: your instructions, plus a medication schedule they can check off.

04

Update anytime

Edit your office's post-operative instructions in Google Docs and the change is live immediately. Nothing to redeploy, nothing to reprint.

At the front desk

Three ways a patient gets set up.

Your front desk opens Kickstart, picks the procedure and medications, and generates that patient's own code. How it reaches them is whatever suits the visit.

Option 01

Scanned in the lobby

The patient scans the code off the screen before they leave. Their instructions and schedule are already on their phone by the time they reach the car.

Fastest — nothing to carry
Option 02

Printed and handed over

Print that patient's own one-page handout and give it to them. It carries their code, how to use the schedule, and your clinic's name and phone number.

Good for a caregiver who isn't in the room
Option 03

Emailed to them

Staff send the link from your practice's own email, with the message pre-filled. The address is typed in your mail program — it is never entered into Postopify.

Check your office's policy before emailing procedure details

What the printed handout looks like

One page, generated for that patient. Their code, six steps for using the schedule, how to save it to a home screen so dose alarms work, and your practice's name and number at the bottom — plus a plain statement that the app runs on their own phone and their medication logs stay there.

Patient data

Patients can fill in their details. We just never see them.

A patient can enter their own name, date of birth and pharmacy — it makes the page theirs, and it's there if they need to call someone. That information stays on their phone. It is never sent to us, and we could not produce it if asked.

Stays on the patient's phone

Entered by them · never transmitted
  • ▪Their name, if they choose to enter one
  • ▪Date of birth, if they choose to enter one
  • ▪Their pharmacy's name and phone number
  • ▪Separate profiles, when one person is tracking for two patients
  • ▪Which doses they've checked off

Reaches our servers

The complete list — nothing else is sent
  • →A short practice code, such as demo
  • →A randomly generated device identifier, not derived from anything personal
  • →The device's time zone
  • →The date of surgery
  • →Which medications are being tracked, and at what times
  • →A Telegram chat identifier — only if the patient connects reminders
No name, date of birth, phone number, email address, medical record number, insurance detail or chart data ever leaves the patient's device — and nothing identifying appears in a link or a QR code. A post-op appointment added to their calendar is a generic entry the patient titles themselves.
Your clinical content stays yours. Instruction documents live in your practice's own Google Drive, not on a server you don't control. Editing a document changes what patients see; deleting it removes the procedure. Postopify reads those documents — it does not own them.
